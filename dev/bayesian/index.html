Exact Bayesian Gaussian estimation

fit_bayesian_gcomp fits a Gaussian outcome regression with a declared Normal–inverse-Gamma prior. Coefficients have a joint Student-t posterior after integrating residual variance. The prior covariance of coefficients conditional on residual variance is sigma2 * covariance; the constructor's shape > 1 restriction ensures finite marginal prior covariance. Scientific prior scales are supplied explicitly and are never estimated from outcomes. The conjugate parameterisation follows Murphy's Gaussian analysis.

using CausalTargeted, DataFrames, LinearAlgebra
prior = ConjugateGaussianPrior(mean = zeros(3),
    covariance = 4.0 * Matrix{Float64}(I, 3, 3), shape = 3.0, scale = 2.0)
fit = fit_bayesian_gcomp(data, :Y, :A, [:W]; prior)
policy = smooth_bounded_logit_shift_policy(0.0, 1.0)
effect = bayesian_policy_contrast(fit, data, policy; deltas = [0.0, 0.2])

The default numeric design is intercept, treatment, then adjustment columns. Categorical columns use the fitted schema. A custom fixed basis can include interactions. The result describes :policy_vs_observed: the mean fitted outcome under a policy minus that under the observed exposures on the supplied target rows. Target rows, basis and policy constants are conditioned on. For a DiscreteTreatmentPolicy, factual and assigned treatment levels must occur in the fitting data; numeric levels follow the same support rule as categorical levels. A target subset may omit a fitted level without removing that level from the policy's available support.

The entire grid shares one coefficient posterior. Its covariance therefore retains dependence between shifts, including legitimate rank deficiency. For unknown variance use Student-t quantiles with 2 * shape posterior degrees of freedom. The Student-t scale matrix and covariance are different quantities. These are model-based credible intervals, with no double-robustness claim. Existing EIF inference and its eligibility checks remain separate.

Missingness and adjustment

Missing outcomes require explicit Gaussian MAR handling. Fit the observed-data likelihood; predictive draws of missing outcomes are separate from the posterior of a mean effect. Missing treatment or adjustment values are unsupported. Select handle_missing=:mar in the fit and request a draw separately with predictive_missing_outcomes(fit, data; rng). A custom fixed basis has signature (predictor_data, treatment_values) -> matrix, retaining its column meanings. With observation_variance=..., inference conditions on known residual variance and the coefficient posterior is Gaussian rather than Student-t.

graph_identification_status records whether a matching certificate establishes the query and actual adjustment set; without one it is :user_assumed. outcome_model_status is always :user_assumed, for both default and custom bases. A graph certificate does not establish regression adequacy. Interactions do not themselves change the adjustment set. The conservative summary identification_status stays :user_assumed for a supplied basis, while the validated graph status and certificate remain available. Prior regularisation can make rank-deficient models computable without demonstrating identification.

Cross-fitted nuisance prediction

BayesianGaussianLearner(; prior) is an optional candidate in the existing Super Learner. It fits each training fold separately and returns posterior mean predictions. Its fitted state is distinct from a full-sample g-computation fit. Coefficient covariance does not enter the existing EIF standard error.

Validation

Independent analytic fixtures test posterior location, scale, covariance and quantiles. The package test/stress/bayesian_calibration.jl script separately reports prior-predictive calibration and fixed-parameter coverage, with Monte Carlo uncertainty. Neither favourable simulation results nor a proper prior guarantee validity under misspecification or poor overlap.
