Choosing an approach

This page maps common biological study designs, exposure types, and sample sizes to the right estimator in CausalTargeted. It assumes you already have a causal graph or adjustment set; if you are still deciding which covariates to adjust for (or exclude), see CausalDynamics.jl.

Why modified treatment policies and targeted learning?

Biological exposures are frequently continuous (parasite load, hormone concentration, nutrient dose, temperature) or repeated over time. Asking "what if every individual had zero parasites?" often violates positivity: in some ages, seasons, or habitats, zero exposure never occurs, so any estimator is forced to extrapolate beyond the data (Petersen et al., 2012).

A modified treatment policy (MTP) asks a realistic, data-supported question instead: "how would the population mean outcome change if each individual's observed exposure were shifted by $\delta$ (or scaled, or raised to a minimum threshold)?" (Díaz & van der Laan, 2012; Díaz et al., 2023).

Standard regression models assume a rigid functional form (such as linearity with no interactions). Targeted learning (TMLE / LMTP) avoids that rigidity by combining flexible cross-fitted machine learning (Super Learner; van der Laan, Polley & Hubbard, 2007; Zheng & van der Laan, 2011; Chernozhukov et al.,

  1. with a targeting step that removes plug-in bias for the specific causal

estimand of interest (van der Laan & Rubin, 2006; van der Laan & Rose, 2011).

Practical checks before interpreting an estimate

Three checks separate a defensible observational estimate from an artefact:

  1. Match complexity to sample size (recommend_run_options). Biological datasets often have tens to low hundreds of units rather than tens of thousands. recommend_run_options(n; engine = :lmtp) selects fold counts and lean Super Learner libraries (SMALL_N_SL_LEARNERS) appropriate for $n$ (Phillips et al., 2023; see Small-n checklist).
  2. Check positivity (positivity_report). Even a shift policy can push individuals into exposure regions with few comparable observations. Inspect effective sample size and weak_support / unsupported_shift flags before interpreting large shifts (Petersen et al., 2012; Díaz et al., 2023).
  3. Quantify sensitivity to unmeasured confounding (sensitivity_report). No adjustment set protects against an unmeasured confounder. Partial-$R^2$ sensitivity reports how strongly an omitted variable would need to explain both exposure and outcome to overturn the result (Cinelli & Hazlett, 2020).

Minimal workflow: small-n shift curve with diagnostics

using CausalTargeted, StableRNGs

# Synthetic cohort: baseline confounder W, continuous exposure A, outcome Y
df, _ = simulate_linear_mtp(150; rng = StableRNG(101))
opts = recommend_run_options(size(df, 1); engine = :lmtp)

grid = run_lmtp_grid(
    df, :A, :Y;
    baseline = [:W],
    deltas = [-0.5, 0.0, 0.5],
    folds = opts.folds,
    learners_outcome = opts.learners_outcome,
    learners_trt = opts.learners_trt,
    parallel = false,
    positivity = opts.positivity,
    simultaneous = false,
    rng = StableRNG(102),
)

rep = positivity_report(df, :A; deltas = grid.delta)
sens = sensitivity_report(grid.est[end], grid.se[end]; n = size(df, 1))
